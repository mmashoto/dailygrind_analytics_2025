Operational Recommendations
1. Align Staffing with Transaction-Driven Demand
Sales performance is primarily driven by transaction volume, not order value.
Increase staffing levels during historically high-volume periods (mid-year and peak months such as August) to:
reduce queue times,
maintain service speed,
prevent lost sales during rush periods.
During lower-volume months, optimise staffing schedules rather than reducing operating hours to preserve service consistency.
Operational Impact: Higher throughput during peak hours without sacrificing customer experience.
2. Optimise Operating Hours Around Proven Demand
Transaction spikes indicate concentrated demand windows, not evenly distributed sales throughout the day.
Use hour_of_day and time_of_day trends to:
extend staffing coverage during peak morning and midday periods,
reduce overstaffing during consistently low-traffic hours.
Operational Impact: Lower labour cost leakage while maintaining peak-hour readiness.
3. Protect Revenue During Seasonal Slowdowns
The late-year decline shows that volume drops can materially impact revenue even when AOV is stable.
Introduce volume-stimulating tactics during slower months:
time-based promotions (e.g. early-morning or mid-afternoon offers),
bundled coffee + pastry deals,
limited-time seasonal drinks to encourage repeat visits.
Operational Impact: Stabilises transaction counts during off-peak periods without discounting core products aggressively.
4. Maintain Pricing Discipline and Menu Simplicity
Stable AOV suggests pricing and menu structure are well-aligned with customer expectations.
Avoid frequent price changes that may introduce unnecessary volatility.
Focus on:
ensuring consistent product availability,
highlighting higher-margin premium drinks during peak hours.
Operational Impact: Preserves margin stability while allowing revenue growth through volume.
5. Inventory Planning Based on Volume, Not Spend
Since each transaction represents a single order, inventory demand scales with number of transactions, not basket size.
Use transaction volume trends to:
forecast ingredient usage more accurately,
prevent stockouts during high-traffic periods,
reduce wastage during slower months.
Operational Impact: Better cost control and fewer service disruptions.
6. Use Monthly Metrics as Operational Control Signals
Track Sales Revenue, Transaction Volume, and AOV as operational “health checks.”
Sharp declines in transaction volume should trigger immediate review of:
staffing adequacy,
operating hours,
external factors (weather, load shedding, nearby competition).
Operational Impact: Faster, proactive decision-making instead of reactive adjustments.
